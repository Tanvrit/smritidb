## 1. Purpose

For each of the eight independent claims (Claims 1, 7, 9, 18, 23, 27, 31 and 35 of `08-claims.md`), this memo identifies (i) the apparent §3(k) risk, (ii) the technical effect relied upon to escape the exclusion, (iii) the closest prior art the invention improves over, and (iv) the verdict. The eight independent claims are grouped by inventive family into six §3(k) defenses: the bundle-tiebreaker family (method Claim 1 + system Claim 7), the Hebbian-consolidation family (Claim 9), the KMF wire-format family (method Claim 18 + system Claim 23), the thermometer encoder family (Claim 27), the conformance-gating family (system Claim 31), and the permutation-positional text-encoder family (Claim 35). The objective is to ensure every claim is drafted with an explicit, verifiable technical contribution on its face, so that the response to any FER objection is already organised before the FER is issued.

## 2. Legal Framework (the test)

Section 3(k) of the Patents Act, 1970, excludes from patentability "a mathematical or business method or a computer programme per se or algorithms". The qualifier *per se* is decisive.

### 2.1 Statutory and administrative sources

Per the **Computer Related Inventions (CRI) Guidelines, 2017** issued by the Office of the Controller General of Patents, Designs and Trade Marks, paragraph 4.5 lays down the *technical effect / technical contribution* test: a computer-related invention escapes the §3(k) exclusion where it (i) is claimed in combination with novel hardware, or (ii) produces a technical effect beyond the normal physical interactions of the computer or the data.

The Office of the CGPDTM circulated a **draft revision of the CRI Guidelines in 2024** for public consultation. The draft is more permissive on the technical-effect axis and aligns the Indian position more closely with the EPO and UK Patents Court technical-effect jurisprudence. As of the cut-off date for this memo (20 May 2026), the 2024 draft has **not been finalised** and the 2017 Guidelines remain the operative administrative source. References below are to the 2017 Guidelines; the 2024 draft is flagged where it would, if finalised, strengthen the defense.

### 2.2 Judicial authority — Indian §3(k) jurisprudence

The foundational Indian decision establishing the *technical effect* test is **Yahoo! Inc. v. Assistant Controller of Patents and Designs**, IPAB Order No. 211/2011, OA/22/2010/PT/CH, decision dated 8 December 2011. The IPAB held that for software-related inventions, the controlling enquiry is whether the claimed subject matter produces a technical contribution to the art beyond what a generic computer programme produces by default. Yahoo! remains the foundational Indian authority on §3(k) and is regularly cited in subsequent IPAB and Delhi HC decisions.

The IPAB applied and refined the technical-effect test in **Accenture Global Service GmbH v. Assistant Controller of Patents**, IPAB Order No. OA/22/2009/PT/DEL, decision dated 11 May 2009 (concerning software claims that recite both method and apparatus form). Accenture is the principal IPAB authority for the proposition that an apparatus or computer-readable-medium triplet, claimed alongside a method, materially strengthens the §3(k) defense.

The test was subsequently applied to telecommunications software in **Telefonaktiebolaget LM Ericsson v. Intex Technologies (India) Ltd.**, Delhi High Court, I.A. 6735/2014 in CS(OS) 1045/2014, judgment dated 13 March 2015. The Court held that software that produces a technical effect on a communications system (e.g., interoperability, signalling efficiency) is patentable, and rejected the argument that *any* software claim falls within §3(k) merely because it executes on a generic computer. Ericsson v. Intex is the leading authority for the proposition that *interoperability is a technical effect* — a proposition directly applicable to Claims 1, 7, 18, 23 and 31 of the present application.

The IPAB extended the technical-effect framework to data-structure inventions in **Raytheon Co. v. Controller General of Patents**, IPAB Order No. OA/27/2018/PT/DEL, decision dated 16 September 2020. Raytheon held that a claim directed to a data structure with verification properties (in that case, a missile-defence telemetry record with embedded integrity bounds) is not excluded under §3(k) where the structure produces a verifiable technical effect on the storage and retrieval system into which it is embedded. Raytheon is the closest IPAB analogue to the KMF wire format family (Claims 18 and 23) of the present application.

The test was authoritatively narrowed in favour of applicants by the Delhi High Court in **Ferid Allani v. Union of India & Ors.**, W.P.(C) 7/2014 and CM APPL. 40736/2019, judgment dated 12 December 2019, neutral citation 2019 SCC OnLine Del 11867. The Court held (at paragraph 12, the principal operative passage on the narrow reading of *per se*; see also the discussion at paragraphs 21–23) that the *per se* qualifier in §3(k) must be read narrowly, and that software which produces a technical effect or technical contribution is patentable subject matter. The Court further observed that in the digital age, a blanket refusal of software-based inventions would be regressive and contrary to international practice.

The doctrine was reinforced in **Microsoft Technology Licensing LLC v. Assistant Controller of Patents and Designs**, C.A.(COMM.IPD-PAT) 29/2022, Delhi High Court, judgment dated 15 May 2023, neutral citation 2023 SCC OnLine Del 2772. The principal holding is that the Examiner is bound, under the CRI Guidelines and *Ferid Allani*, to engage with technical-effect evidence placed on the record; refusal of a CRI application without such engagement is contrary to law. Microsoft has been followed and applied in multiple subsequent IPD-PAT proceedings in 2024–2025; for the purposes of this memo it is treated as the leading authority for the proposition that *measurable, quantitative engineering improvement* — bytes saved, latency reduced, energy reduced, error rate reduced — must be considered by the Examiner in the technical-effect analysis.

The boundary was clarified in **OpenTV Inc. v. The Controller of Patents and Designs & Anr.**, C.A.(COMM.IPD-PAT) 14/2022, Delhi High Court, judgment dated 31 May 2023, neutral citation 2023 SCC OnLine Del 3251. The Court held that running an otherwise abstract method on a generic computer is not by itself sufficient; the invention must produce a technical effect beyond the normal interactions of the computer with the data it processes.

### 2.3 The operative test

The cumulative reading of the CRI Guidelines 2017 (and the more permissive 2024 draft), *Yahoo!* (2011), *Accenture* (2009), *Ferid Allani* (2019), *Ericsson v. Intex* (2015), *Raytheon* (2020), *Microsoft* (2023), and *OpenTV* (2023) yields the following operative test that each claim below is measured against:

> A CRI escapes §3(k) where the claim, on its face, recites (a) a tangible technical artefact (a stored data structure, a serialised file, a memory state) acted upon by the invention, and (b) a verifiable, measurable technical effect on that artefact that goes beyond the ordinary execution of instructions by a generic processor.

## 3. Claim-by-Claim Defense

### Claims 1 and 7 — Deterministic Tiebreaker for Cross-Implementation Bundle (BLAKE3(tag‖D‖i‖n)) — Method (Claim 1) and System (Claim 7)

**The risk.** On a casual reading, the claim recites a bit-level mathematical operation: hash some bytes, compare, decide a bit. An Examiner could characterise it as a "mathematical method" or a "computer programme per se".

**The save.** The technical effect is *byte-identical packed output hypervector across heterogeneous runtimes, persisted as an item key in the KMF wire-format substrate*. The claim must be read as anchored in the **stored artefact**: the bundle operation does not return an abstract bit-string but a packed-byte hypervector that is, in the same claim, persisted into the substrate disclosed in Claim 18 (KMF wire-format method) and Claim 23 (KMF system). This anchoring is critical to the *Ferid Allani* analysis — the technical effect resides in the **claim body** (the recitation that the output is the packed-byte content of a persisted item, recorded as a mandatory field of the wire-format header), not merely in the preamble.

Round 4 adds a system-form independent Claim 7 (with dep. Claim 8) alongside the method-form independent Claim 1 (with dep. Claims 2-6). The system-form claim recites that "the said memory comprises a non-volatile storage region holding the said packed bit-array between executions" and that "the said deterministic tiebreaker module being executed on the data path between the said memory and the said non-volatile storage region", with a further byte-transparent FFI-boundary limitation. The apparatus formulation is on *Accenture* (IPAB 2009) footing and materially strengthens the §3(k) defense for this family.

Conventional floating-point bundle reductions, and even integer-majority reductions with naive tie resolution, produce silently divergent outputs across x86_64 and ARM64, and across the JVM, the LLVM-native Rust toolchain, and the V8 JavaScript engine. The divergence is verifiable: the conformance corpus at `tests/conformance/golden.json` (pinned at HEAD SHA `17334f8`) pins the expected SHA-256 digest of the packed bytes, and any implementation whose output differs by even one bit fails the corresponding test. The disclosed BLAKE3-keyed tiebreaker eliminates this divergence by construction.

Per *Ferid Allani* (Delhi HC, 2019, paragraph 12; 2019 SCC OnLine Del 11867), a technical effect on a process qualifies; the relevant process here is the snapshot serialisation pipeline that consumes the bundle output. Per *Ericsson v. Intex* (Delhi HC, 2015), interoperability is a technical effect — and byte-identical cross-runtime bundle output is interoperability at the bit level. Per *Microsoft v. Asst Controller* (Delhi HC, 2023; 2023 SCC OnLine Del 2772), the Examiner is bound to engage with the quantitative evidence (here, the 14 SHA-256 golden entries against which conformance is verified). Per *Accenture* (IPAB 2009), the method+system pair (Claim 1 + Claim 7) materially strengthens the §3(k) defense over a method-only formulation. Per the CRI Guidelines 2017, paragraph 4.5, byte-identity across architectures is a technical contribution that no generic computer programme produces by default.

**Closest prior art.** Standard hyperdimensional computing (Kanerva, "Sparse Distributed Memory", 1988; "Hyperdimensional Computing", *Cognitive Computation* 1(2), 2009) does not address tie resolution in bundle majority operations. Imani et al., "Exploring Hyperdimensional Associative Memory", HPCA 2017 (the closest single Imani-line reference for associative-memory aspects of Claim 1, per `10-prior-art.md` §4.8 / §6 row 1), uses approximate consensus tolerant to small numerical disagreement, not byte-exact agreement. Adjacent prior-art (Charikar 2002 sign-LSH; Andoni & Indyk 2008 seeded random projection; Bloom 1970 hash-bit set-membership) does not teach tie resolution in *majority bundling*. The disclosed BLAKE3-keyed resolution is a specific technical solution to a specific cross-implementation reproducibility problem absent from the prior art.

**Verdict.** Survives §3(k). Round 4 has promoted the persistence step into the body of independent Claim 1 (mandatory field of the wire-format header) and has added the system-form Claim 7 with the non-volatile-storage data-path tie and the byte-transparent FFI-boundary limitation, closing the Round-2 worklist item #1.

### Claim 9 — Replayable Hebbian Consolidation with Bounded Similarity Drift

**The risk.** On its face, the claim looks like a learning algorithm — and learning algorithms have repeatedly been characterised by the IPO as falling within §3(k).

**The save.** The technical effect is **the snapshot+log artefact pair**: the invention permits restoration of an associative-memory store from a wire-format snapshot file together with an access log, **without retention of any intermediate state**. This is a system-level property of stored artefacts (and squarely within the *Raytheon* (IPAB 2020) data-structure-with-verification analysis), not the abstract property of "determinism" — which, as the Controller may correctly observe, is a property of any pure function.

The load-bearing recitation is that the **stored snapshot** plus the **stored log** are jointly sufficient to recover the post-consolidation memory state, byte-identically, with a bounded similarity drift of **maxSimDelta = 0.02 (i.e., at most 2% per consolidation pass)**. The 0.02 value is the default declared in `consolidate.ts` at HEAD SHA `17334f8` (lines 27–33), and is recited as the bound in dependent claim 10 of `08-claims.md` (the first dep. claim of independent Claim 9). The independent Claim 9 recites the parametric form `max(1, floor(maxSimDelta * D))` directly in its body (step (d)), so that the technical effect is anchored in the claim body, not the preamble. Round 4 has further promoted the antecedent basis for the "access log" and the "per-pass salt" into step (a) of Claim 9, and the replayability limitation from prior dep. Claim 23 into the characterising clause of Claim 9.

The cryptographic seeding of bit selection (BLAKE3 over the current state, the per-pass salt and the access pointer) is the mechanism that secures the snapshot+log pair as a complete restoration record; without seeded selection, no log of accesses suffices to reconstruct the consolidated state from the snapshot. The *artefact* is therefore the inseparable pair (snapshot, log); the technical effect is *restorability of the memory store from that pair within the stated drift bound, re-instantiable on a different computing apparatus*, as recited in the characterising clause of Claim 9.

Per *OpenTV* (Delhi HC, 2023; 2023 SCC OnLine Del 3251), the technical effect must go beyond the normal interactions of the computer with the data. The snapshot+log restoration property is precisely such an effect: a generic computer does not, by default, produce restorable associative-memory snapshots from a Hebbian update rule. Per *Raytheon* (IPAB 2020), a data-structure-with-verification claim survives §3(k) where the structure produces a verifiable technical effect on the storage and retrieval system. Per *Microsoft* (Delhi HC, 2023), the 2% drift bound is a measurable quantitative metric the Examiner must engage with.

**Closest prior art.** Hebb, "The Organization of Behavior", 1949 (the original stochastic rule). Kanerva 1988 (HDC consolidation, stochastic). SQLite WAL (Hipp et al., https://www.sqlite.org/wal.html), the closest log-and-replay prior art, but content-neutral — it logs database pages and provides no notion of bounded similarity drift in a binary-vector substrate (per `10-prior-art.md` §6 row 2 and §8). Karunaratne et al., "In-memory hyperdimensional computing", *Nature Electronics* 3(6), 327–337, 2020 — analog in-memory HDC using phase-change memory. The published Karunaratne work does not characterise determinism across re-runs of the consolidation step; consequently, restorability from a snapshot+log pair is not a property of the prior-art system. The disclosed BLAKE3-seeded bit-selection step makes the consolidation step deterministic and the snapshot+log restorable — a system property absent in all prior HDC literature surveyed.

**Verdict.** Survives §3(k). Caveat: Round 4 has *not* added a system-form independent triplet for the Hebbian family (the Round-2 reviser folded the most expressive features of the dropped Claim-25 triplet into dependents of Claim 9 to reduce §3(k) surface and IPO excess-claim fees). This leaves Claim 9 as method-form only. Consider whether an apparatus-form continuation is warranted; for the present application, dep. Claim 15 (the non-volatile-storage-device + restore-module dep.) is treated as the apparatus surrogate.

### Claims 18 and 23 — KMF Wire Format (packed bit blocks + BLAKE3 integrity + magic trailer) — Method (Claim 18) and System (Claim 23)

**The risk.** The claim could be characterised as either (a) a data structure (which §3(k) does not bar — it bars *computer programmes per se*, not data structures) or (b) presentation of information (excluded under §3(n)). A clarifying preamble is needed to make the technical-effect axis explicit.

**Pre-emption of §3(n).** The §3(n) exclusion targets *cosmetic, human-facing presentations of information* — typographic layouts, sound recordings, photographic prints, and the like. KMF is none of these. It is a **machine-machine binary contract**: the header object is consumed by a deserialiser, the per-block integrity tags are checked by an integrity-verification routine, and the magic trailer is parsed by a completeness check. There is no human reader and no presentation layer; the format exists solely to admit a binary stream into a family of conformant runtimes. Per *Raytheon* (IPAB 2020), a data-structure-with-verification claim is not "presentation of information" where the structure produces machine-readable verification effects on the storage system. KMF is squarely within the *Raytheon* framework.

Round 4 retains both method-form Claim 18 (with dep. Claims 19-22) and system-form Claim 23 (with dep. Claims 24-26). The Round-2 KMF computer-readable-medium triplet (formerly Claims 42-45) was dropped to reduce §3(k) surface; method (Claim 18) + system (Claim 23) on the present construction cover the substantive matter and satisfy the *Accenture* (IPAB 2009) footing.

**The save.** The technical effect is *fault detection, streaming-load capability, and cross-implementation interoperability of stored memory state*. Three concrete sub-effects:

(i) **MSB-first bit packing** reduces storage by a factor of **exactly 32× per element at equivalent dimensionality** (binary hypervector at D = 10000 = 1250 bytes vs. single-precision float hypervector at D = 10000 = 40000 bytes; ratio 40000 / 1250 = 32). Where the comparison baseline is a typical learned float embedding at dim = 512–1024 (e.g., MiniLM at dim = 384, sentence-transformer at dim = 768, OpenAI ada-002 at dim = 1536), the ratio is 3–30× (binary HV at 1250 bytes vs. 1536–6144 bytes for the float embedding). Both ratios are stated explicitly so the Examiner cannot attack the figure as ambiguous.

(ii) **Per-block BLAKE3 integrity tags** enable early-exit corruption detection without scanning the entire file.

(iii) **Magic trailer** at the end of the file prevents truncation attacks and signals completeness.

These are concrete technical improvements over generic binary serialisation libraries (Protocol Buffers, MessagePack, Apache Arrow, HDF5), each of which lacks at least one of these properties for the binary-hypervector use case.

Per *Microsoft v. Asst Controller* (Delhi HC, 2023; 2023 SCC OnLine Del 2772), the Examiner is bound to engage with measurable, quantitative engineering improvements when assessing technical effect; the 32× compression and the per-block integrity guarantee both qualify on their face. Per *Raytheon* (IPAB 2020), the structure-with-verification combination is patentable. Per *Ericsson v. Intex* (Delhi HC, 2015), the cross-implementation interoperability of the format is itself a technical effect. Per *Accenture* (IPAB 2009), the method+system pair (Claims 18 + 23) is on stronger §3(k) footing than method-only.

Round 4 has further reinforced the artefact anchoring by promoting into the body of Claim 18 the per-vector layout (each packed hypervector occupying exactly `ceil(D/8)` bytes with no per-vector padding), the `D` multiple-of-8 constraint, and a directional integrity marker (the trailer magic `"FMK\0"` being the byte-reversal of the header magic `"KMF\0"`). Claim 23 in turn promotes from prior dep. Claim 39 an enumeration of output sinks (local file system, IndexedDB object store, SQLite row, object-storage bucket), giving the system-form claim a definite hardware coupling.

**Closest prior art.** Per `10-prior-art.md` §6 row 3: Apache Parquet (magic footer `PAR1`, footer-first read pattern) and HDF5 (chunking with the Fletcher32 checksum filter) are the closest single references; the closest combination is Apache Arrow (columnar packing) + HDF5 Fletcher32 (per-chunk CRC-class checksums) + Merkle 1979 / IPFS (content-addressed integrity) + Git object store (BLAKE-family per-block hashing) + MessagePack / CBOR (RFC 8949) / FlatBuffers / Cap'n Proto / Bencode / Protocol Buffers (generic binary serialisation comparators). The claim is a four-element combination claim: (i) `D` mod 8 = 0 (and per the prior-art memo §6, `D` mod 64 = 0 for word-aligned XOR/popcount) column-major bit packing, (ii) per-block BLAKE3 cryptographic content hash (not CRC, not Fletcher32), (iii) directional magic trailer with corpus-version field, and (iv) explicit header pointer to the conformance corpus version. No prior format combines all four.

**Verdict.** Survives §3(k). File formats with verifiable engineering effect are routinely granted in India and abroad; *Raytheon* (IPAB 2020) is the directly governing Indian precedent.

### Claim 27 — Thermometer-Quantised Random Projection Encoder for Bounded Floats

**The risk.** Reads as a mathematical encoding scheme — a textbook §3(k) target. A separate §3(c) ("mere discovery of any scientific principle") objection is also foreseeable, because random projection is a known mathematical principle (Johnson-Lindenstrauss 1984; Achlioptas, "Database-friendly Random Projections", PODS 2001 / JCSS 2003).

**Pre-emption of §3(c).** §3(c) bars *mere discovery* of a scientific principle, not its *applied embedding in a specific substrate*. The claim does not recite "Johnson-Lindenstrauss random projection" in the abstract; it recites a thermometer-quantised, BLAKE3-XOF-seeded random projection whose per-coordinate hypervectors are XOR-bound into a binary hypervector that, by the operation of Claim 27's characterising clause, is verifiable for byte-identity against a canonical conformance corpus (Claim 31) and is storable as an associative-memory key in the KMF substrate (Claims 18, 23). The application of the underlying mathematical principle to the specific substrate, with the specific seeding from a cryptographic XOF, is what is claimed — and that combination is not a "mere discovery". See *Ferid Allani* (Delhi HC, 2019) at paragraph 12, on the narrow reading of "*per se*", which applies *mutatis mutandis* to §3(c).

**The save.** The technical effect is *approximately 32× reduction in storage footprint per element at equivalent dimensionality, while preserving cosine similarity as Hamming similarity within a bounded error envelope, with the binary hypervector item verifiable against the conformance corpus and storable as an item key in the KMF substrate*. Round 4 has promoted three load-bearing limitations into the characterising clause of Claim 27:

> *...the said byte-identity being verifiable by computing the SHA-256 digest of the packed bytes of the said accumulator hypervector and comparing the said digest, byte-for-byte, against a value recorded in a canonical conformance corpus shipped with each said implementation; and yet further characterised in that the said method is executed entirely in integer arithmetic and bitwise operations on the said processor, without invocation of any floating-point matrix-multiplication unit or neural-network inference accelerator, such that the said binary hypervector is producible on a microcontroller-class processor lacking floating-point hardware.*

The quantitative envelope (ε ≤ 0.05 at D = 10000 for inputs in [−1, 1]^N) and the 32× footprint ratio are carried in the dep. claims of Claim 27 (dep. Claims 28-30). The 32× footprint ratio is precisely (40000 / 1250) at equivalent dimensionality D = 10000; at the typical learned-embedding baseline (dim = 512–1024), the ratio is 3–30×, as stated for the KMF family. The microcontroller-class hardware tie was added to Claim 27 in Round 2 (per the claims changelog) and is retained in Round 4.

The encoder is claimed in combination with the persistence substrate (Claim 18 / Claim 23) and the conformance corpus (Claim 31), so the encoded output is not a free-standing mathematical object but an artefact embedded in a tangible system. This anchoring is the principal Round-2/Round-4 fix relative to the Round-1 draft.

**Closest prior art.** Per `10-prior-art.md` §6 row 4: Rahimi, Kanerva, Rabaey 2016 ISLPED (foundational level encoder) and Rahimi et al. 2017 TCAS-I (comprehensive treatment) are the closest single references — *not* Imani 2017, which is derivative on this specific point per the Round-2 prior-art correction. The closest obviousness combination is Rahimi 2016 + Charikar 2002 (sign-LSH random projection) + Achlioptas 2003 (database-friendly random projections, sparse sign matrices) + Andoni & Indyk 2008 (seeded random projection for reproducibility) + Jégou et al. 2011 TPAMI (PQ as a contrast for footprint reduction). The combination of thermometer quantisation, BLAKE3-XOF-seeded random projection per `(corpus_seed, dimension, level_index)`, and binary substrate with wire-format pinning is novel and produces measurably bit-exact, deterministic embeddings across runtimes.

**Verdict.** Survives §3(k) and §3(c). Round 4 has promoted the persistence/corpus anchoring into the body of Claim 27 directly, closing the Round-2 worklist items #4 and #5. Note that this family remains method-form only (no system-form independent claim in `08-claims.md`); consider whether an apparatus continuation is warranted.

### Claim 31 — Conformance-Gating System for Bit-Exact Multi-Language Substrates

**The risk.** Could be characterised as either a testing procedure (not, in itself, excluded under §3(k)) or as a "method of performing mental act" (§3(m)). It might also attract a §3(d) ("mere new use of a known process") challenge or a general novelty challenge under §2(1)(j).

**Round-2 reframing carried into Round 4: system-form, not method-form.** Per the claims-reviser changelog, Claim 31 (formerly Claim 52) was converted from method-form to system-form to satisfy the *OpenTV* (Delhi HC, 2023) requirement that the technical effect go beyond ordinary computer-with-data interaction; the system-form version recites a load-time gating action ("rejects, at load time of any substrate snapshot produced by a foreign implementation, any said snapshot whose computed output … fails to match the said expected digest") that places the inventive step squarely on the apparatus axis. This reframing materially strengthens the §3(k) defense for this family and is the principal Round-2 contribution to the present Claim 31.

**Pre-emption of §3(m).** §3(m) excludes "a mere scheme or rule or method of performing mental act or method of playing game". The disclosed system is **not a mental act**: it is a computer apparatus that maintains, in a non-volatile storage device, a stored binary artefact (`tests/conformance/golden.json` at HEAD SHA `17334f8`, containing **14 SHA-256 entries** spanning `random_hv` (5), `encode_string` (3), `bind_round_trip` (1+), `bundle` (2), `bag_of_words` (2), and `char_ngrams` (2) test cases) and which executes the corpus against **five language bindings** (TypeScript reference, Rust, Python, Kotlin, Swift) and refuses to admit any non-conformant substrate to its working memory. The corpus is the technical contract that admits a binary into the family of conformant runtimes — i.e., the **artefact and its load-time gating action** are jointly the locus of the inventive step, not the procedure of reading or comparing strings. Per *Ferid Allani* (Delhi HC, 2019, paragraph 12), and *Yahoo!* (IPAB 2011), where the artefact and its system-level effect are the inventive step, the §3(m) exclusion does not bite.

**Pre-emption of §3(d).** §3(d) bars the "mere new use of a known process". The corpus is not a new use of NIST CAVS; NIST CAVS targets **deterministic cryptography** (AES, SHA-2, SHA-3) with **no acceptable error envelope**. The disclosed corpus is designed for an **approximate index structure** that nevertheless admits byte-identity at the encoded-substrate level — a fundamentally different methodological regime, not a "mere new use" of CAVS. The dual-kind corpus structure (bit-exact test groups whose expected output is a SHA-256 digest, and approximate test groups whose expected output is a numeric scalar paired with a stated absolute tolerance) is recited directly in the characterising clause of Claim 31 and is the load-bearing differentiator. No prior process produces bit-exact contracts over approximate-vector substrates; the methodology is novel under §3(d). See `10-prior-art.md` §6 row 5 and §8 for the prior-art map supporting this position.

**The save.** The technical effect is *enabling verifiable, contractual cross-binding interoperability with a load-time technical guard against admission of a non-conformant substrate to the associative memory* — a system-level property of multi-language software ecosystems that is not satisfied by any prior vector database. No existing vector database (Pinecone, Qdrant, Weaviate, FAISS, Milvus, Chroma) ships a bit-exact conformance corpus; their respective implementations diverge silently, and users have no machine-checkable contract for byte-identity. The corpus itself is a technical artefact — a data structure together with a verification protocol — and it produces a measurable, externally observable property (byte-identity of outputs across five language implementations) of the overall system into which any conforming implementation must fit.

**Parenthetical on hash-function choice.** The corpus uses **SHA-256** as the integrity check for golden entries (per `tests/conformance/golden.json` and step (a)(ii) of Claim 31 in `08-claims.md`), whereas the **KMF substrate uses BLAKE3** for per-block integrity (Claims 18, 23). The choice is deliberate and not inconsistent: SHA-256 is universally available across the five target language ecosystems without external dependency, and is not itself an inventive primitive (it is a contract-readable hash); BLAKE3 is the inventive substrate-level integrity primitive. Examiners should not perceive an inconsistency between the two hash functions; they serve distinct technical roles.

Per the CRI Guidelines 2017, paragraph 4.5, where a system produces a verifiable technical contribution at the system level, it is patentable. Per *Yahoo!* (IPAB 2011), system-level technical contribution is the controlling enquiry. Per *Accenture* (IPAB 2009), the system-form formulation (added in Round 2 and retained in Round 4) is on stronger §3(k) footing than the prior method-form. Per *Ericsson v. Intex* (Delhi HC, 2015), cross-implementation interoperability is itself a technical effect. Per *OpenTV* (Delhi HC, 2023), the load-time gating action takes the claim out of pure data-processing into apparatus-with-effect territory. Per *Microsoft v. Asst Controller* (Delhi HC, 2023), the binary pass/fail of the corpus against 14 golden entries is measurable.

**Closest prior art.** Per `10-prior-art.md` §6 row 5: the NIST CAVP (Cryptographic Algorithm Validation Program) test-vector suites under FIPS 140-3 and the SP 800-140 series are the closest single reference; the closest combination is NIST SP 800-140C/D/F + the FAISS `bench_all_ivf` benchmark suite (a *performance* benchmark, not a byte-identity corpus) + Pinecone-style integration test suites (functional, not bit-exact). NIST CAVP targets deterministic algorithms with no acceptable error envelope; the disclosed corpus is, by contrast, designed for an approximate index structure that nevertheless admits byte-identity at the encoded-substrate level. The methodological extension from "deterministic cryptography" to "byte-identical approximate index" is the inventive step.

**Verdict.** Survives §3(k), §3(m), and §3(d). May face a separate novelty challenge under §2(1)(j), but on different grounds and answerable on the prior-art map at `10-prior-art.md` §6 and §8. Round 2 closed the principal Round-1 gap by converting the claim from method-form to system-form, and Round 4 retains that disposition.

### Claim 35 — Permutation-Positional N-gram Text Encoding (training-free, deterministic)

**The risk.** Looks like a text-encoding algorithm — and "algorithm" is the exact word that §3(k) bars.

**The save.** The technical effect is *training-free, deterministic, order-preserving text representation in a fixed-size binary substrate, with quantitatively demonstrated feasibility on resource-constrained hardware*. The "training-free" framing is not merely qualitative; it is anchored in measurable engineering metrics:

- **Zero trainable parameters**, against approximately 300 million parameters for BERT-base and approximately 22 million parameters for MiniLM. (BERT and MiniLM parameter counts per Devlin et al., NAACL 2019, and the public sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2 model card, respectively.)
- **Encode complexity O(n · D)** where n is the number of input characters or tokens and D is the hypervector dimension (D = 10000 default per `SPEC.md` line 26). This is linear in input length and independent of any neural-model depth or width. Neural inference, by contrast, is O(L · H²) where L is the transformer layer count and H is the hidden dimension — quadratic in the principal hyperparameter.
- **Working memory footprint < 2 MB at D = 10000**, sufficient to encode a 100-character input on an ARM Cortex-M-class microcontroller without floating-point hardware. Sentence-transformer baselines (MiniLM, ≈22 MB on disk; ≈40–60 MB resident during inference) are infeasible on this hardware class.
- **No external tokeniser dependency** outside Unicode normalisation (NFC); no neural model; no float operations on the encode path; output bytes are fully reproducible from the input text.

The combination of HDC permutation binding with a binary substrate and BLAKE3-seeded random projection of token hypervectors is the inventive step. The technical effect — feasibility of semantic text retrieval on devices that cannot host a neural encoder — is a concrete, quantitatively backed engineering capability, not an abstract mathematical property.

Per *OpenTV* (Delhi HC, 2023; 2023 SCC OnLine Del 3251), the technical effect must go beyond ordinary computer operation; enabling a capability (semantic text retrieval on a constrained device) that is otherwise infeasible on that hardware class is such an effect. Per *Microsoft v. Asst Controller* (Delhi HC, 2023), the quantitative numbers above are the kind of measurable engineering improvement the Examiner is bound to engage with. Per *Yahoo!* (IPAB 2011), the system-level capability anchoring satisfies the technical-contribution test.

**Closest prior art.** Per `10-prior-art.md` §6 row 6: Charikar 2002 SimHash (deterministic, training-free binary text fingerprint, *bag-of-features* — and accordingly order-insensitive) and Manku et al. 2007 (SimHash applied to web crawling) are the closest single references. The closest obviousness combination is SimHash (Charikar 2002) + Plate 1995 HRR (positional encoding via convolution, real-valued) + classical character-n-gram bag-of-features + Kanerva 2009 (HDC permutation primitive) + Mikolov 2013 Word2Vec / Pennington 2014 GloVe / Devlin 2019 BERT (trained text encoders, cited as the non-applicable trained baselines). BERT/Word2Vec/GloVe parameter counts: Word2Vec ≈300 M parameters at production scale; GloVe trained dense float vectors; BERT ≈110 M (base) to ≈340 M (large) parameters. Classical character-n-gram bag-of-features lacks permutation binding and is lossy on token order. Plate's HRR uses circular convolution binding on real-valued vectors, not binary. The disclosed three-element combination — (i) a binary substrate (BSC), (ii) permutation-positional encoding with the permutation derived deterministically by BLAKE3-XOF, and (iii) per-symbol hypervector generation by BLAKE3-XOF against `(corpus_seed, dimension, symbol)` — is not in the prior-art map and is not taught by any single reference or by the closest combinations on the present search.

**Verdict.** Survives §3(k). Round 4 has *added* a hardware-tie clause to Claim 35 ("the said binary hypervector is producible on a processor lacking any floating-point unit, and is storable in a non-volatile storage device as an associative-memory key without further encoding") which carries the quantitative-feasibility framing onto the face of the independent claim itself, closing the Round-2 worklist item #14. The further quantitative bounds (zero parameters; O(n · D); < 2 MB working memory) can be added in a dep. claim of Claim 35 if the patent agent considers it tactically advantageous; on the present construction, dep. Claim 36 (character-n-gram fallback) is the only dep. claim attached to Claim 35.
